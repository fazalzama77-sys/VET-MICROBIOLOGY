Lymphoid System & the Two Arms of Immunity

t01–t03

Innate vs acquired immunity table

Improves on repeatNoYes
  • Classify acquired immunity — always draw this 2 × 2:
    Active natural = recovery from infection · Active artificial = vaccination · Passive natural = colostral/maternal antibody, transplacental · Passive artificial = antiserum, antitoxin, hyperimmune serum.
  • Active — slow onset, long-lasting, has memory, needs a functioning immune system. Passive — immediate, short-lived (2–3 weeks), no memory, useful in an emergency (tetanus antitoxin, snake antivenin).
  • Species point that gets asked: the ruminant, horse and pig placenta is epitheliochorial/syndesmochorial — no maternal IgG crosses in utero, so the neonate is agammaglobulinaemic at birth and depends entirely on colostrum absorbed by pinocytosis in the first 6–12 h (gut "closure" by 24–36 h). In dogs and cats ~5–10% crosses the endotheliochorial placenta; in primates and rabbits (haemochorial) most crosses. Failure of passive transfer is the single commonest cause of neonatal septicaemia in calves and foals.
  • Other terms: herd immunity, local/mucosal immunity (IgA), species, breed and individual resistance, adoptive immunity (transfer of sensitised lymphocytes).

Lymphoid organs mechanism

Two species facts examiners love: the pig has an inverted lymph node — medulla outside, cortex inside, with afferent lymph entering centrally; and the bursa of Fabricius, a dorsal cloacal sac, is the site of B-cell maturation unique to birds (bursectomy abolishes antibody production but leaves cell-mediated immunity intact — the experiment that separated the two arms). Haemal nodes occur in ruminants and filter blood rather than lymph.

Antigens, Adjuvants & Immunoglobulins

Antigen vocabulary rapid-fire

AntigenA substance that combines specifically with antibody or a lymphocyte receptor. Immunogen = an antigen that can also induce a response. All immunogens are antigens; not all antigens are immunogens
Epitope / determinantThe small part actually bound — 5–8 amino acids or sugars. Linear (sequential) vs conformational. Valency = number of epitopes per molecule
ParatopeThe antigen-binding site of the antibody
HaptenLow-molecular-weight, antigenic but NOT immunogenic alone; becomes immunogenic when coupled to a carrier protein. Examples: penicillin, picryl chloride, drugs causing allergy. Landsteiner's classic work
MitogenNon-specifically activates whole populations of lymphocytes. T cells — PHA, Con A · B cells — LPS (endotoxin), pokeweed mitogen acts on both. Used in the lymphocyte transformation test to assess cell-mediated immunity
SuperantigenBridges MHC-II to the TCR Vβ outside the peptide groove → activates up to 20% of all T cells → cytokine storm. TSST-1, staphylococcal enterotoxins, streptococcal pyrogenic exotoxin
AdjuvantEnhances the response to an antigen without being antigenic itself — see next module
FactorRule for good immunogenicity
ForeignnessThe more phylogenetically distant, the better — self is normally tolerated
Molecular size>10 kDa good, >100 kDa excellent; <5 kDa usually poor
Chemical complexityProteins > polysaccharides > lipids > nucleic acids; aromatic residues help
DegradabilityMust be processable by APCs — D-amino acid polymers are not immunogenic
Dose & routeOptimal dose; too little or too much gives tolerance. Route order: intradermal > subcutaneous > intramuscular > intraperitoneal > intravenous > oral
Host factorsSpecies, breed, age, genotype (MHC haplotype — immune response genes), nutrition, stress

The immunoglobulin molecule mechanism

  • Basic unit = 2 identical heavy + 2 identical light chains = H₂L₂, ~150 kDa, Y-shaped, held by disulphide bonds.
  • Fab = fragment antigen binding, monovalent — cannot precipitate or agglutinate. F(ab′)₂ is bivalent and still cross-links. Fc = crystallisable, carries all the biological (effector) functions and the carbohydrate.
  • Variability terms: Isotype = class/subclass differences (constant region, all members of a species) · Allotype = allelic differences between individuals · Idiotype = differences in the variable region/paratope of one clone. CDRs (hypervariable regions), three per chain, form the paratope.

Antibody production & diversity rapid-fire

Side-chain theoryEhrlich — cells bear pre-formed receptors; antigen selects and the cell overproduces them. Partly correct in spirit
Template (instructive) theoriesBreinl & Haurowitz, Pauling — antigen moulds the antibody. Disproved: denatured antibody refolds with the same specificity, and antibody is made after antigen is cleared
Natural selection theoryJerne, 1955 — antigen selects a pre-existing natural antibody
Clonal selection theoryBurnet, 1957 — the accepted one. Each lymphocyte is pre-committed to one specificity; antigen selects the matching clone, which proliferates into plasma cells and memory cells; self-reactive clones are deleted in fetal life ("forbidden clones")
Generation of diversityTonegawa's somatic recombination — V(D)J rearrangement by RAG-1/RAG-2 (heavy chain uses V-D-J, light chain V-J) · combinatorial pairing of any H with any L · junctional diversity and N-nucleotide addition by TdT · somatic hypermutation in the germinal centre giving affinity maturation. Together these produce >10⁹ specificities from a few hundred genes
Class switchingSame V region joins a different CH gene → specificity unchanged, effector function changed; driven by cytokines and CD40-CD40L
Idiotype networkJerne — anti-idiotypic antibodies regulate the response; the internal image can act as a vaccine

MHC, Receptors, Antigen Presentation, Complement & Cytokines

t11–t15

MHC & antigen presentation — one diagram, two pathways mechanism

MHC is polygenic and the most polymorphic gene system known, is co-dominantly expressed, and is inherited as a haplotype block. Consequences to quote: it determines graft acceptance or rejection, it sets the immune response (Ir) gene control of vaccine responsiveness, and MHC associations underlie several autoimmune and disease-resistance traits (for example BoLA associations with mastitis resistance).

γδ T cells Recognise antigen with little or no MHC restriction, sit in epithelia, and are unusually abundant in young ruminants and pigs — a favourite short-note in veterinary papers. Signal 1 = TCR–peptide–MHC; signal 2 = co-stimulation CD28–B7; signal 1 without signal 2 gives anergy. CTLA-4 delivers the inhibitory signal.

Complement — three pathways, one lytic end mechanism

Five functions — the standard answer 1 Cytolysis by the MAC · 2 Opsonisation (C3b) · 3 Chemotaxis (C5a) · 4 Anaphylatoxin and inflammation (C3a, C5a) · 5 Immune-complex clearance and B-cell activation (C3d–CR2). Complement is heat-labile — 56 °C for 30 min destroys it, which is why serum must be inactivated before a complement fixation test, and why guinea-pig serum is the standard source of fresh complement.

Tolerance & autoimmunity rapid-fire

Immunological toleranceSpecific unresponsiveness to an antigen that would otherwise provoke a response. Induced most easily in the foetus or neonate, by very high or very low antigen doses, by soluble non-aggregated antigen and by the oral or intravenous route
Central toleranceIn thymus and bone marrow. Negative selection — clonal deletion of strongly self-reactive lymphocytes; AIRE drives expression of peripheral self-antigens in the thymus. Also receptor editing in B cells
Peripheral toleranceAnergy (signal 1 without signal 2), clonal deletion, suppression by Treg (IL-10, TGF-β, CTLA-4), and immunological ignorance of sequestered antigens
Classic demonstrationFreemartin cattle and dizygotic twin calves sharing a placental circulation become permanent blood chimeras and tolerant of each other's tissue — the natural experiment. Medawar's neonatal skin-graft work made the same point
How autoimmunity arises1 Release of sequestered antigen (lens, sperm, myelin, thyroglobulin) after trauma or infection · 2 Molecular mimicry — a microbial epitope resembles self (Campylobacter and Guillain-Barré) · 3 Alteration of self by drugs or haptens · 4 Polyclonal activation by superantigens or LPS · 5 Failure of Treg or of AIRE · 6 Aberrant MHC-II expression on non-APCs · 7 Genetic (MHC haplotype) and hormonal (female bias) predisposition
Veterinary autoimmune diseasesAutoimmune haemolytic anaemia and immune-mediated thrombocytopenia (dog) · Pemphigus foliaceus (dog, horse — commonest autoimmune skin disease) · Systemic lupus erythematosus (dog, ANA positive) · Equine recurrent uveitis (moon blindness, leptospiral mimicry) · Myasthenia gravis (anti-AChR) · Lymphocytic thyroiditis (dog) · Rheumatoid arthritis · Autoimmune orchitis and thyroiditis
DiagnosisCoombs' test, ANA, LE-cell test, direct immunofluorescence on a skin biopsy, response to immunosuppression
